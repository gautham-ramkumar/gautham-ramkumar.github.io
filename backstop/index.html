Backstop — Runtime failure prediction for VLA policies

A supervisory layer that watches a frozen Vision-Language-Action policy and stops a run that is going wrong earlier than a timeout would.

Abstract

A Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy maps camera images and a natural-language instruction straight to robot actions. It generalises well, and it fails silently: nothing in the action stream says that the gripper is about to close on empty air. Backstop is a supervisory layer that watches a frozen policy while it runs and decides whether to trust what the policy does next. On a recorded SmolVLA corpus in LIBERO, a guard of action-chunk disagreement combined with a stopwatch, with both triggers set from good runs only, ends failing runs 13.4 steps (0.67 s) sooner than a stopwatch alone, at a 4.3% held-out false-stop rate, and catches every failure across 20 random splits. A separate open-vocabulary detector gate stops 28 of 30 runs whose instructed object is absent, before the arm moves. Recovery — lift, back off, and retry — does not help this policy.

The problem, in two clips

Same task, same frozen policy, adjacent starting scenes. Nothing in the policy’s output distinguishes them. Both are from the recorded corpus, LIBERO-Spatial task 0, seeds 0 and 1. The camera is the agentview render at 20 Hz. The failure clip is shown at 2× speed.

On the LIBERO-Spatial clean baseline the policy succeeds on 70 of 100 episodes. All 30 failures stall until the 280-step cap. That pattern is what makes a stopwatch a strong baseline, and what a learned-looking signal has to beat on time, not on failures caught.

Method

The policy is HuggingFaceVLA/smolvla_libero, frozen, on LIBERO (MuJoCo, Franka Panda). Backstop never modifies or retrains it. Rollouts are recorded once into a LeRobot dataset that keeps the full 50-step action chunk and K = 4 sampled chunks per step. Guard development then runs against that corpus and iterates in seconds.

Signals

Action-chunk disagreement is the spread of K sampled plans under different flow-matching noise. A wide spread means the policy has no confident plan. That signal is the one that made the headline. Temporal self-consistency, progress monitoring, and a learned trust head were also tried; the trust head caught the most failures alone, but as a guard OR stopwatch it saved less time than disagreement (0.47 s vs 0.67 s). Those alternatives are archived.

The metric

Accuracy is the wrong measure: a guard that halts everything catches every failure and is useless. The false-stop budget is 5% of runs that would have succeeded, fixed in writing before any guard existed. Once a stopwatch turned out to catch every failure in this corpus, the headline became time saved over that clock, with both held to the same budget. Rules are set from good runs only, by an order statistic with no search, which bounds the false-stop rate by construction.

Is the failure visible at the onset?

At step t, take the signal’s highest value so far, and compare failures with the successes still running at t. 0.5 is a coin flip. The reviewed onset of a no-grasp or failed-grasp episode is around step 40; there the signal sits at 0.65. By the end of a run, separation is much stronger (AUROC about 0.8–0.9).

Missing-object gate

A per-object open-vocabulary detector (OWLv2) asks, before the arm moves, whether the instructed object is in the first frame. It is reported on its own and is not added to the headline guard. It needs reference frames from the same kind of scene, and it cannot tell some lookalike grocery cartons apart.

Unseen suite (LIBERO-Object)

Thresholds do not transfer. Object-suite good runs are slower and score higher, and the frozen spatial rule stops 17% of them (31 of 83 successes; it detects 16 of 17 failures). Recalibrating from about 40 good runs in the new suite, with no failures needed, brings the false-stop rate back under 5%. The signal carries over; its level does not.

Recovery does not help this policy

When the guard fires, the arm opens, lifts 10 cm, backs off 8 cm and settles, then the policy is allowed to try again. That motion rescued 1 of 19 failures and spoiled 1 good run, a paired change of 0 (McNemar p = 1.0). The policy returns to the same stall a median of 33 steps after each recovery.

Limitations

  • One policy (SmolVLA) on LIBERO in simulation. No real hardware, no policy fine-tuning, no comparison across architectures.
  • Every natural failure of this policy is a stall. That makes a stopwatch strong and the signals’ contribution small. A setting whose failures end early — a collision, a drop — would give the signals more to do; the metric is defined so that would show up.
  • Onset labels are one reviewer’s judgement, so lead time is a secondary metric.
  • Recovery was tested with one motion on one policy. A recovery that changes the policy’s situation more than a short move, or re-wording the instruction, is untested.
